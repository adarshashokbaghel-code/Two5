# Research and editorial policy

Applies to public pages on Two5 that mention cognition, ageing, dementia, or training. Last updated 27 October 2026. This policy describes a writing standard. It does not describe an ethics board, a laboratory, or a study that is already running.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the hierarchy that matches the question.

- For “does this intervention change a health outcome in a defined group?”, prefer official guidance and systematic reviews, then the randomized trials those reviews rest on, then observational studies as signals rather than proof. A single positive paper does not override a review that includes it and still calls the evidence weak.
- For “how does this puzzle work?”, a worked example with one correct answer is the right evidence. A meta-analysis is not.
- For “what does Two5 do today?”, the product is the source. Do not fill gaps with research language.

Do not copy a result from healthy older adults onto people with dementia, or from people with mild cognitive impairment onto college students, unless the source itself makes that comparison.

## Clinical boundaries

Ordinary Two5 games, including future scored rounds, are not diagnostic tools and not medical treatments.

Do not label a person as cognitively impaired from a score. Do not show an unvalidated dementia-risk score. Do not infer a medical condition from how someone plays. Do not collect identifiable health information on the waitlist or in ordinary gameplay.

Personal worry about memory belongs with a qualified clinician. Say that. Do not give individualized medical advice.

## Research with older adults

If a real study is proposed later, it would need qualified collaborators, ethics review, informed consent, accessible procedures, data minimization, a plan for participants whose decision-making is impaired, validated outcomes, a preregistered protocol where that is appropriate, and reporting of null results.

None of that is in place. The collaboration page is an email address. A reply is not a partnership, and it is not enrollment.

## Citations

Name the organisation or the paper, the year, and the URL that was checked. Prefer the guideline or the paper over a news rewrite, and use the news release when that is the document you actually read. Do not invent a page number, a statistic, or an author you did not verify.

On 27 October 2026 the WHO publication URL returned 200. The two NIA pages returned 200 to a normal browser request. The DOI links for Simons and Owen redirected to the publisher pages. The NIH news URL is the public release titled “Cognitive speed training over weeks may delay the diagnosis of dementia over decades,” dated 10 February 2026. A datacenter request to that URL received a bot challenge; the title and the speed-plus-booster finding were confirmed from the public release text the same day.

## Updates

When a cited page changes, or a review supersedes one of these sources:

1. Change the sentence in the article.
2. Change this table in the same commit.
3. Set the article `updated` date to that day. The sitemap uses that date.
4. Leave `published` alone.
5. If the new evidence is uncertain, say so in the article. Do not wait for a flattering result.

Puzzle guides do not need this table unless they start making a health claim. They should not.
